# Questions for a lawyer

Date: 2026-11-09. Written by an AI (Claude, through Claude Code) for the project owner, who is also not a lawyer. Each question is short, with the background a lawyer needs. Facts are from `legal-audit.md` in this folder. The owner's country is not yet known and decides which law applies (`owner-checklist.md` A1).

**The project in four sentences.** Harness Design Studio is an offline desktop and command-line tool for designing spacecraft wiring harnesses. It is a personal, non-commercial project of one owner (GitHub account `0h3xn4`). Most of the code, tests and documentation were generated by an AI (Claude, through Claude Code) under the owner's direction. It lives in a public fork of WireViz (GPL-3.0). 20 releases with packages for Ubuntu and Windows (and, for four old releases, macOS) are public.

---

## Q-1. Copyright in AI-generated code, and effect on the GPL and the disclaimer

**Background.** The owner set requirements, made decisions, reviewed and approved. Claude wrote most of the code. The plan is to claim copyright "only to the extent the applicable law allows" and to license "all parts in which copyright exists" under GPL-3.0-only (file `NOTICE`).
**Questions.** (a) In the owner's country, can the owner hold copyright in the code, tests and documents? (b) If parts are not protected, does the GPL notice still do any harm or lose any effect for the parts that are protected? (c) Do the GPL sections 15 and 16 (no warranty, limit of liability) still protect the owner for unprotected parts? (d) Is the NOTICE wording adequate?

## Q-2. Releases 1.0.0 to 1.17.0 published without a licence

**Background.** For 20 public releases the tool's own texts said "no licence is granted" (`LicenseRef-Proprietary`). The repository is public, so GitHub's terms may give others some rights (view, fork). GitHub itself labels the repository GPL-3.0 because of the root `LICENSE` (WireViz's). Some releases carry the AI-written phrase "the tool is used internally and never shared". The owner will either (a) add licence and notices to old release assets or (b) publish a new release and mark the old ones as superseded.
**Questions.** (a) What is the legal status of the old releases and of copies already downloaded? (b) Is the grant "GPL-3.0-only" in the new release enough, or must the old releases be changed or withdrawn? (c) Which option, (a) or (b), is better for the owner?

## Q-3. Are the disclaimer and intended-use texts enough?

**Background.** The planned texts are: "Provided as is, without warranty of any kind. Check all results before you rely on them. See sections 15 and 16 of the GPL-3.0." and "Harness Design Studio supports harness design work. Every harness design must be checked by qualified engineers under your own processes before it is built or flown." They go into the README, the About dialog, a first-run notice, every generated output, the website and every release note. The tool's outputs also contain fields such as "status released", "checker" and "approver" that can look like an approval.
**Questions.** (a) Are these texts and these places enough in the owner's country? (b) Should anything be added or removed (for example a statement on use in regulated or safety-critical work)? (c) Does a "release" or "approved" label in the output create any risk even with the notice?

## Q-4. Is "free software, no commercial activity" kept in practice?

**Background.** Decision L-2: no prices, paid support, donations, sponsorship, ads or services. The app and website collect no personal data. The aim is to stay outside the EU Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 and the Cyber Resilience Act (EU) 2024/2847, which exclude free and open-source software supplied outside a commercial activity, and outside consumer-law duties.
**Questions.** (a) Is that conclusion right for this project and the owner's country? (b) Does the fact that the owner is also the maintainer, publishes on GitHub and offers no support change it? (c) Do GitHub issues answered "at the owner's discretion" or accepted contributions affect it? (d) Does the owner's possible use of the tool at work (if any) matter?

## Q-5. Imprint and privacy duties for a personal project website

**Background.** A static site on GitHub Pages at the default `github.io` address. No cookies, no browser storage, no third-party requests, no forms, no analytics. GitHub receives visitors' IP addresses when it serves the site. Contact is a GitHub profile or issue. No real name or address is planned on the site.
**Questions.** (a) Does the owner's country require an imprint (for example an Impressum under section 5 DDG in Germany) for a free, non-commercial software page, and what must it hold? (b) Is a privacy notice that names GitHub as the host enough? (c) Does the European Accessibility Act or a national accessibility law apply?

## Q-6. Reproducing ECSS and ESCC requirement text

**Update 2026-11-10.** The owner had the requirement text removed from the repository (D-221); it remains in the Git history. Questions (a) and (b) now concern only that history and our IDs and clause numbers in `compliance/assessment/`; (c) and (d) are unchanged. The owner declined a lawyer review (owner-checklist B8).

**Background.** `compliance/requirements/*.csv` hold about 1,900 rows of requirement text copied from ECSS-E-ST-40C, ECSS-Q-ST-80C, ECSS-Q-ST-30-11C, ECSS-E-ST-20-07C and ESCC-3901, up to 600 characters per row. The PDFs are not in the repo. The CSVs are in the public repo but not in the packages. The tool also ships optional "standard profiles" with table values from the standards. ESA holds the copyright of ECSS documents. The ECSS reproduction clause was not read at source.
**Questions.** (a) May the owner publish this text, and under which conditions? (b) May short extracts (IDs, clause numbers, titles) stay? (c) May the table values in the profiles stay, and with which notice? (d) Does the reference to the standards in outputs (requirement IDs on checks) need a disclaimer of compliance?

## Q-7. Product liability and the limits of a liability disclaimer

**Background.** Results may be used for real hardware. Several texts in the app say "Independent check", "No problems", "released". The owner is a private person. GPL section 16 limits liability "to the extent permitted by applicable law".
**Questions.** (a) How far does the disclaimer protect against a claim from a user or a third party in the owner's country? (b) Is the wording "self-check" and "no problems found by the checks that could run" enough to avoid a misleading impression? (c) Is any insurance or other step advisable?

## Q-8. Export control

**Background.** The tool designs wiring harnesses for spacecraft. Example projects use invented values and are labelled so. No ITAR or EAR marking, no mission, customer or programme name was found in the repository. The tool is public and downloadable worldwide.
**Questions.** (a) Can publishing software and example data for spacecraft design be export-controlled, in the owner's country or under US rules, even if it holds no real data? (b) Does open publication change the answer? (c) Is a classification or a notice needed?

## Q-9. Trademarks and the name "Harness Design Studio"

**Background.** The name begins with the word "Harness". Aggregator sites list a USPTO word mark HARNESS in classes 9 and 42 (owner not verified at the register). The CLI of a well-known DevOps company is also named `harness`, and the tool installs a command alias `harness`. No exact "Harness Design Studio" product was found; similar names exist (see `legal-audit.md`, area 9). The registers were not searched by the AI.
**Questions.** (a) Is there a real risk of conflict or of a claim? (b) Should the name or the `harness` alias change before the project grows? (c) Are the non-affiliation and trademark notes enough?

## Q-10. WireViz fork, modified upstream files and GPL scope

**Background.** The repository is a fork of WireViz (GPL-3.0, many contributors). The owner's tool does not copy or import WireViz code (checked by token comparison and history). Twelve of thirteen upstream source files were reformatted and modernised in a pull request, against the owner's earlier decision. The root `LICENSE` is upstream's. The new tool code will be GPL-3.0-only with its own `LICENSE`.
**Questions.** (a) Is a notice of modification enough to meet GPL-3.0 section 5(a) for the changed files, or should they be restored? (b) Is one repository with two code bases under the same licence safe? Does the tool become a derivative work because it writes WireViz-style YAML and lives in the fork? (c) Is the description "does not copy or import WireViz code" accurate enough, given that AI agents read WireViz sources before writing the tool?

## Q-11. Bundled third-party components

**Background.** Packages are PyInstaller bundles with PySide6-Essentials 6.11.2 (Qt 6.11.2, LGPL-3.0 option, dynamically linked, replaceable), Python 3.14, 87 Ubuntu libraries (Linux), and Microsoft VC and UCRT DLLs plus Mesa/LLVM `opengl32sw.dll` (Windows). None of the packages carries notices or a source offer today. The plan is `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt`, source archives attached to each release, and replaceability notes.
**Questions.** (a) Is "source archives attached to each release" a valid way to offer corresponding source for the LGPL and GPL parts? (b) Are the Microsoft redistributable terms met by shipping those DLLs in a free GPL package? (c) Are there open points with FreeType (dual licence), Mesa/LLVM, or the Qt option chosen?

## Q-12. Third parties who may have a claim

**Background.** Some documents (written by the AI) address "your company" and "your IT". A workshop with unnamed engineers gave feedback that is used in the design. One release tag was created by another named person with a company address. The owner states no employer, client or other person has any claim. The owner's answers are in `owner-checklist.md` A2 to A6.
**Questions.** (a) Which evidence should the owner keep to show that the project is the owner's alone? (b) Should the workshop participants confirm in writing that their feedback may be used? (c) If the owner has an employment contract, what should the owner check in it?

## Q-13. History rewrite and personal data

**Background.** A personal mailbox address and a second personal address are in public git history. One third party's name and company address are in a tag. Upstream contributors' addresses are in upstream history. A rewrite would change commit IDs and break forks and links.
**Questions.** (a) Do the owner's privacy rights in the owner's country call for a rewrite or only for future care? (b) What duties does the owner have towards the third party whose address is in the tag, if any? (c) Is a deletion request to GitHub the better route?
