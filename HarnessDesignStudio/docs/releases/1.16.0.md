This release is about drawing the block diagram. Labels stay on their line, devices are easy to tell apart, connecting two devices feels like picking up a cable, and the windows no longer lie on top of themselves on a real screen. **One thing changes for every project, on purpose: the block diagram the tool writes (`system/block_diagram.svg` and `system/block_diagram.pdf`) and the diagram you export from the editor (SVG, PDF and PNG) now show the new looks of the devices.** Nothing else that is written changes, and no number changes. The calculations and the 39 design rules are as in 1.15.0.

### The main changes

- **The written block diagram changes for every project.** Each device card now has a band of the colour of its family on its left edge, a badge with the picture of its kind, then the ID and the name, and a key to the kinds the project has sits beside the key to the lines. This is in `system/block_diagram.svg`, in `system/block_diagram.pdf` (written by every `export`) and in **File > Export diagram** (SVG, PDF and PNG). The files are paper, so they use the colours of the light theme. The harness drawings, the tables, `harness_overview`, `export.json` and every number are as in 1.15.0, apart from the tool version in the stamps and the model hash that follows from it, and `manifest.json` lists the new hash and size of the two block diagram files.
- **Devices are easier to tell apart.** In the window a device has the picture of its kind (battery, reaction wheel, star tracker, transceiver and so on, 20 in all), a band in the colour of its family and a badge shape, and the zoomed-out boxes carry the same band and badge. Colour is never the only sign: the picture, the shape, the tooltip, the **Inspector** and the legend say it too. The tool guesses the kind from the name, the ID, the subsystem and the links, and in the **Inspector**, under **Subsystem**, you can set **Kind** by hand (with **Undo**). A project that sets no kind keeps byte-identical files and the same model hash; one that sets a kind has it in its model hash. The page *Telling devices apart* in the user manual has the key.
- **Labels stay on their line.** The pill of an interface, the label of a connection and the connector boxes at its ends slide only along their own line, stay attached when you move a device or the line is routed again, and **Reset label and line positions** puts them back.
- **Connecting devices feels like picking up a cable.** Press on a port and drag: a cable follows the pointer, the ports and devices that can take it light up, and the plug snaps to a port. In Expert mode there is a port for each connector; in Guided and Quick mode there is one for each device. Let go on a port, on a device (the tool picks the connector that fits) or on empty ground (the cable stays as a loose end that you can pick up again). A port that cannot take it says why. You can also pick up the end of a connection and plug it in elsewhere, as one undo step. Devices are no longer trapped in the panel: they can go past the edge and above or left of the first lane, the diagram grows as you carry them and scrolls at the edge of the window.
- **Dialogs and windows no longer lie on top of themselves on a real screen.** The *Add device* window was too short for its text on a real display, so fields lay over notes and the banner over the buttons. Every dialog now keeps the height its text needs at its width, forms stay quiet until your first change, the **Harness** and **Devices** tabs scroll in height only, and a test looks at every dialog at five sizes, in both themes and with larger fonts.
- **A rare crash of the editor is fixed.** The editor could close without a message when a design check finished while the diagram redrew (a Python 3.14.6 hazard in a garbage collection on a worker thread). Python's cyclic garbage collector now runs on the main (GUI) thread only. This is a deliberate change of behaviour; nothing changes on the screen.

### Upgrading

There is no migration: the file format keeps its schema version (1) and a project from 1.15.0 or older opens as it is. There is one new optional field, the `kind` of a device (`logical/units/<subsystem>.json`), written only when you set a kind; a tool older than 1.16.0 refuses a device that has one until the key is taken out. The marks in `diagram/marks.json` are migrated when the diagram is drawn (a label that was a free offset becomes a place along its line) and are written in the new form at the next save; 1.15.0 or older sets that form aside as unreadable and places the labels itself. Loose ends of a cable are only on the screen and are never saved. After upgrading, the next `export` writes a different block diagram for every project, so a comparison of the written files shows `system/block_diagram.svg`, `system/block_diagram.pdf` and `manifest.json` as changed; every other written file differs only in the tool version and the model hash that follows from it.

### Known limits

- **Nobody has looked at any of this on a real display.** The window was tested with Qt's offscreen platform only. The first look on a real display is what found the overlapping widgets that the dialog fix addresses, so please look again and report what you see.
- **Smaller limits.** A loose end of a cable is a draft and is not saved. In Guided and Quick mode a device card has one port for the whole device. There is no keyboard path to a port (the Connect tool and the window *Link connectors* are the keyboard way). A few buttons of the rail are a few pixels narrower than their text at some sizes. Nine colours cannot all stay apart for every kind of colour blindness, which is why the picture and the shape are there too.
- **Two things were not done in 1.15.0 and stay not done**, because they need engineering input: an AC or skin-effect model of the voltage drop, and a mass model that counts splices and backshells.
- **No compliance and no sign-off is claimed.** The owner has not signed this release off. The packages are not signed (Windows SmartScreen shows *Windows protected your PC*: **More info**, then **Run anyway**), the configuration values are placeholders and the parts are example parts.
